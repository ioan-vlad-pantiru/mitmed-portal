# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Inferred from the existing application: clients of the MitMed clinic use the portal on phone or desktop to see and manage appointments, book a therapy, complete their personal and medical information, review care records, sign required declarations, and see payments. Reception and clinical staff use separate admin screens.

## Product Purpose

The portal gives a client a calm, private, practical home for their relationship with the clinic. Success means a client can immediately understand their next step, complete required preparation, and manage routine actions without contacting reception.

## Positioning

It combines appointment logistics, care continuity, consent, and client-owned information in one Romanian-language portal for a physical-therapy clinic.

## Operating Context

Clients generally arrive with a concrete need: confirming a session, booking another session, checking remaining package sessions, or completing requested information. The portal contains personal and medical information, so clarity, restraint, accessibility, and trust matter more than decorative novelty.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Existing server actions and API contracts must remain unchanged. The portal is responsive, supports client appointment cancellation, booking, profile and medical-history updates, consents, payments, care records, and an optional Google-review prompt. Romanian is the primary UI language.

## Brand Commitments

MitMed name and logo assets are established. The existing palette uses teal, sky blue, warm off-white, and restrained orange accents.

## Evidence on Hand

Real product content and user data are supplied dynamically by the API. The repository contains MitMed logo assets in `public/brand/` and the existing portal implementation; no client testimonials or external proof should be invented.

## Product Principles

- The next useful action is always visible first.
- Medical and personal information is presented with calm discretion.
- The interface should reduce reception work, not create another task for the client.
- Routine tasks stay familiar and easy to complete on a phone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Preserve semantic controls, keyboard access, responsive layouts, readable contrast, and reduced-motion support.
